# Third-Party Developer Monetization: Where to Build to Earn Money (2026)

> Deep research compiled June 2026. This document maps the realistic landscape of
> platforms and ecosystems where an independent ("third-party") developer can build
> products and earn money — **not restricted to iOS apps**. Each section covers the
> opportunity, the economics (revenue share / fees), realistic earnings, and what
> kind of skills/effort it rewards.

---

## TL;DR — How to Read This

The honest reality across **every** platform below: the median product earns
very little, and a small top percentage earns the vast majority. Success is far
less about which platform you pick and far more about:

1. **Distribution** — can you get in front of buyers without paying for every click?
2. **Niche depth** — narrow, painful, underserved problems beat broad, crowded ones.
3. **Recurring revenue** — subscriptions/credits compound; one-time sales reset to zero.
4. **Owning the customer** — selling direct (your own site + Stripe) keeps 90%+ vs.
   marketplaces that take 15–70%.

The biggest 2026 shift: **AI lowered the cost of *building*, which means the
moat moved entirely to distribution and niche expertise.** Picking a hyper-niche
you understand is now the single highest-leverage decision.

## 1. Mobile App Stores (iOS + Android)

**The market.** The Apple App Store generated ~**$152B** and Google Play ~**$88B**
in app spend. iOS users spend nearly **2.1x more per app** than Android users, so
for paid/subscription products iOS usually wins; Android wins on raw reach.

**The hard truth.** The **median app makes under $50/month** after a year, and only
**~17% of apps ever reach $1,000/month**. Revenue ranges from ~$10/mo for small apps
to $10M+/mo for apps with 1M+ MAU — almost entirely a function of retention and ARPU.

**Economics.** Apple/Google take **15%** if you earn under $1M/year (the small-business
program), **30%** above that. Small developers' App Store earnings rose **76% from
2021–2024**, so the small end is genuinely growing.

**What works in 2026.**
- **Subscriptions + hybrid models** dominate: ~60% of top-grossing apps now mix ads,
  subscriptions, and IAP.
- **Business / B2B apps** are the most underrated category — they retain and monetize
  best. The opportunity: B2C-quality polish applied to a B2B workflow.
- Hyper-casual games need massive scale (ad-driven); mid-core/strategy can monetize
  small audiences at high ARPU via IAP.

**Best fit for you if:** you want a consumer-facing product and can nail retention
and a subscription hook. Treat it as a marketing problem as much as an engineering one.

## 2. Micro-SaaS (Web Apps) — The Best Risk/Reward for a Solo Developer

If you have "no bar on technical skill," this is usually where a developer's skills
convert most directly into recurring money — because **you own the customer and the
platform takes almost nothing** (just ~3% Stripe fees).

**The market.** Micro-SaaS is projected to grow from **$15.7B to $59.6B by 2030**
(~30%/yr). Micro-niches grew **340%** vs. broad platforms (Gartner Q4 2025). A typical
micro-SaaS runs with 1–5 people, generates **$50k–$3M/year**, and hits profitability
in 1–2 years.

**The math that makes it attractive:** at **$99/mo × 100 customers = $9,900 MRR**.
You only need **100–500 customers** in a niche to build a real business — which means
even "small" markets are big enough.

**High-margin niches (proven willingness to pay):**
- **Vertical CRMs** — fitness coaches, photographers, real-estate agents, freelancers.
  Domain-specific beats generic.
- **AI content tools** — repurposing, meeting notes, niche email writers, competitor
  analysis. People pay $19–$99/mo.
- **Creator-economy tools** — YouTubers, podcasters, newsletter writers, course creators.
- **Developer / AI-ML ops tools** — 70–85% margins, strong willingness to pay.
- **Healthcare compliance / legal tech** — regulatory complexity supports $99–$500/mo.

**Best fit for you if:** you want the highest ceiling and the most control. The catch
is you must do your own distribution (SEO, communities, cold outreach). **My top
recommendation to start.**

## 3. Browser Extensions (Chrome / Edge)

A sweet spot for developers: small surface area, fast to build, and you can bill
**outside** the Chrome Web Store — meaning **Google takes 0%** of subscription revenue.

**Economics & earnings.** Well-monetized extensions with **~10,000 users make
$1,000–$10,000/month**. Outliers: Closet Tools ~$42k/mo; GMass reported ~$130k/mo.
Tools like **ExtensionPay** (stores data in your own Stripe) or **Dodo Payments**
(merchant-of-record, handles global tax) make billing trivial.

**The three models that work in 2026:**
1. **Freemium subscriptions** — best for productivity tools.
2. **Usage-based credit packs** — best for AI extensions where each action has a real API cost.
3. **One-time lifetime purchases** — best for narrow utilities (beats subscription fatigue).

**Best fit for you if:** you want the fastest path from idea → paying users with
minimal platform tax. Distribution still comes from the store listing + content.

## 4. E-commerce App Ecosystems (Shopify, etc.)

Building on an established commercial platform means the buyers already have budgets
and a reason to pay (they're running a business).

**Shopify App Store.** You keep **100% of your first $1M lifetime** revenue, then
**85%** above that (15% cut), plus 2.9% processing. Note: in 2025 Shopify changed
the $1M exemption from *annual reset* to *lifetime* — worse for big earners, still
generous for newcomers. **Average developer ~$93k/year; top 25% ~$167k/year.**
Developers have collectively earned **$1.5B+**.

**Best fit for you if:** you understand a merchant pain point (shipping, upsells,
reviews, inventory). B2B buyers churn less and pay more than consumers.

## 5. WordPress Plugins

WordPress powers a huge share of the web, so the addressable market is enormous and
mature.

**Economics.** Selling **direct** (your own site) keeps **90%+** — strongly preferred.
**CodeCanyon** takes **30–70%** (starts ~52.5%, drops to ~32.5% only after ~$78.7k
gross). Average plugin author earns **~$13k/year**, but top performers are huge:
Gravity Forms ~$5.4M/yr (no free version), Barn2 Plugins ~$141k/month from just 16
products.

**Best fit for you if:** you want a proven, deep market and are willing to sell direct
with a freemium funnel rather than rely on a marketplace.

## 6. Desktop Apps (macOS / Windows)

Power users pay well for tools that save them time, and desktop has less subscription
fatigue resistance for genuinely useful utilities.

**Distribution options (Mac).**
- **Direct (DMG) download** — keep **100%**. **66% of US Mac users** install apps
  outside the Mac App Store.
- **Mac App Store** — 15% (<$1M) / 30%, but built-in discovery and trust.
- **Setapp** — subscription bundle that pays developers **up to 90%** (70% guaranteed),
  Spotify-style by usage. Great for niche utilities that struggle to be discovered alone.
- **Paddle** — merchant-of-record for selling indie apps directly (handles tax).

**Best fit for you if:** you build developer tools, productivity, or creative utilities
for a focused power-user audience.

## 7. API & Data Products

If your edge is backend/data engineering, you can sell an API others build on.

**Economics.** **RapidAPI** keeps **~25%** + processing — but note Nokia acquired it
(Nov 2024) and the consumer marketplace has **declined**, pivoting to telco/enterprise.
Selling **direct** (your docs + Stripe metered billing, or tools like Zuplo/Moesif)
keeps ~97% and is increasingly the better path. Indie success stories range from a
modest **$877** to **five-figures/month** for a single well-positioned API.

**Best fit for you if:** you can wrap unique data, a hard computation, or an
infrastructure capability into a clean, well-documented, usage-billed endpoint.

## 8. Digital Products & Templates (Notion, Gumroad, etc.)

The lowest-effort, fastest-validation channel — great for testing demand and building
an audience before committing to a full app.

**Economics & earnings.** **Gumroad** charges **10% (new sellers) down to 2.9%**
(>$1M). Beginners earn **$200–$1,000/mo**; experienced creators in good niches hit
**$10k–$100k+/mo**; Notion templates commonly do **$500–$1,500/mo**, top bundles
$5k–$50k/mo. Pricing typically **$7–$97** per template.

**What sells:** prompt libraries, workflow automations, Notion templates, industry
playbooks, custom GPT configs, AI tool guides. The 2026 sweet spot is **hyper-niche**
("Notion dashboard for independent insurance agents").

**Best fit for you if:** you want quick cash flow and audience-building with near-zero
infrastructure, possibly as a funnel into a SaaS later.

## 9. AI-Native Channels (GPT Store, Claude Skills/MCP, Agent Marketplaces)

The newest frontier — but be clear-eyed: **direct rev-share is small; the real money
is B2B.**

- **GPT Store:** engagement-based payouts average **~$0.03/conversation** → you'd need
  **33,000+ quality conversations** for $1,000/mo. Most creators hit a **$100–$500/mo**
  soft ceiling. Top niche performers: $500–$15,000/mo.
- **Claude Skills / MCP servers:** **free to publish** (no paid tier as of Q2 2026) —
  Anthropic's ecosystem has **4,200+ skills, 770+ MCP servers, 2,500+ marketplaces**.
  The model is **lead-gen**: publish a capability for free, convert to a paid service.
- **The actual playbook in 2026:** smart developers stopped chasing viral consumer
  GPTs and pivoted to **B2B / enterprise internal agents** — a single engagement pays
  **$5k–$20k upfront + $1k–$5k/mo** maintenance. Many publish the *same* capability as
  a Skill **and** a GPT **and** an MCP server **and** a Hugging Face Space.

**Best fit for you if:** you want to ride the newest wave — but monetize it via
consulting/services or by funneling users into your own billed SaaS, not via store payouts.

## 10. Other Platform Marketplaces (B2B SaaS Ecosystems)

Building inside a big B2B platform gives you buyers with budgets:
- **Slack Marketplace** — 2,600+ apps; 6,000+ via MCP; ecosystem revenue $180M (+20% YoY).
- **Salesforce AppExchange / AgentExchange** — unified with Slack; $50M partner fund.
- **HubSpot Marketplace** — now exposes search analytics to app partners (better discovery).
- **Atlassian, Figma (15% native fee), VS Code (no native paid tier — use code-checkout
  workarounds), JetBrains, Zoom, Notion.**

**Best fit for you if:** you already live in one of these tools and feel a recurring
pain you could productize for other teams.

---

## 11. Games & Immersive (Steam, itch.io, Roblox, VR)

High passion, high variance — treat as a creative bet, not a reliable income plan.
- **Steam:** 70% to you after the 30% cut. 2025 was the highest-grossing indie year
  (>$4.5B) but the **top 5 titles captured >$500M** — brutal concentration.
- **itch.io:** flexible/optional revenue share; **most games earn <$100 lifetime**;
  marketed launches $500–$5k; sustained hits $5k–$50k.
- **Roblox UGC/experiences:** Roblox keeps **~50–70%**; top 1,000 creators average
  **$1.3M/yr**, but the long tail earns very little. Now also on **Meta Quest VR**.
- Only **~10–30% of indie devs profit on their first game.**

**Best fit for you if:** you genuinely love game-making and can market — otherwise the
economics are the worst on this list.

## Recommendation: A Concrete Path for You

Given "no bar on technical skill" and the goal of earning money, here's the sequencing
I'd suggest:

1. **Pick a niche you understand**, ideally one with business buyers (they pay and churn
   less). This decision matters more than the platform.
2. **Validate cheaply first** — ship a Notion/Gumroad template, a Chrome extension, or a
   tiny tool, and see if anyone pays. Days, not months.
3. **Build the recurring product** as a **micro-SaaS billed direct via Stripe** (keep ~97%),
   or a **Chrome extension with out-of-store billing** if the workflow lives in the browser.
4. **Use AI channels (Claude Skills / MCP / GPT) as free distribution and lead-gen** into
   that paid product, not as the revenue source itself.
5. **Reinvest in distribution** (SEO, communities, content) — that is the real moat now
   that building is cheap.

**Best single starting point:** a **vertical micro-SaaS or a browser extension**, sold
direct, in a niche where you have unfair insight. Highest margin, highest control,
recurring revenue, minimal platform tax.
